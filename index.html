Kappa History

  • Founded on January 5, 1911 at Indiana University Bloomington
  • 10 Founders
  • Colors – Crimson & Cream
  • Over 150,000 members with 700 undergraduate and alumni chapters in every state of the United States
  • Fraternity website: www.kappaalphapsi1911.com
Alpha Omega, Kappa Alpha Nu, Kappa Alpha Psi
The group was first called Alpha Omega while its formation continued. Alpha and Omega, the Greek alphabet's first and last letters, correlate to Christ and the Founder's relationship and connection to the church. On January 5, 1911, the fraternity was originally known as Kappa Alpha Nu, possibly as a tribute to the Black students of 1903 (the Alpha Kappa Nu Fraternity) who preceded them at Indiana University. Kappa Alpha Nu became the first incorporated Black fraternity in the United States once granted a charter by the Indiana Secretary of State on May 15, 1911. The Greek letter Ψ replaced N. The name was officially changed to Kappa Alpha Psi® on a resolution adopted at the Grand Chapter Meeting in December 1914. This change became effective on April 15, 1915.
Firsts
Second-oldest existing collegiate historically Black Greek letter fraternity and the first intercollegiate fraternity incorporated as a national body. It is the first Greek letter organization to establish its Alpha Chapter on Indiana University's campus. It would be the only Greek letter organization founded with the concept of achievement.
The Journal
The inaugural edition of The Kappa Alpha Nu Journal debuted in April 1914, and it has been printed uninterrupted, except for 1918 and 1919, due to WWI. A frontrunner among Black Greek lettered collegiate organizations to issue a monthly publication. In Memoriam to Brothers who have passed away and transitioned into a place, we refer to as the Chapter Invisible.

Founding roles
Diggs presided as president, while Irvin was assigned as interim secretary of Alpha Omega. Diggs was named permanent chairman; Lee was designated as secretary, and Armstrong as sergeant-at-arms. Irvin served on the fraternity's Incorporation Committee. Edmonds was listed as the Corresponding Secretary in the articles of incorporation of the Grand Chapter of Kappa Alpha Nu. The three freshmen, Asher, Blakemore and Edmonds, were the first initiates of the Alpha of Kappa Alpha Nu.
Elder Watson Diggs
Born in Christian County, Kentucky, on December 23, 1883. Eldest son of three children. Initially referred to as “the Father of Kappa,” he became affectionately known as “the Dreamer” due to his profound vision to create the fraternity. Was a quiet, polished, scholarly, and prolific writer. Following graduation from Indiana State Normal School in the spring of 1908, Diggs enrolled at Howard University in 1909. 1st Grand Polemarch, 1911–1917. Occupation: not stated on the national site.
Byron Kenneth Armstrong
Born April 8, 1892, Westfield, Hamilton County, Indiana. The cousin of 2nd Grand Polemarch, Irven Armstrong. Known as “Boomski.” Was a scholar, imaginative and outspoken. Held teaching positions as a professor at universities in Florida, Indiana, Kansas, Maryland, and Michigan. Served as Dean in Maryland and Oklahoma. Earned an M.A. degree from Columbia University in 1914. Was decreed a Doctor of Philosophy by the University of Michigan in 1940.
John Milton Lee
Born in Danville, Indiana, on September 7, 1890. Third of four children. Founder John Milton Lee was a scholar, loyal, and tireless worker for the growth of the fraternity. Commonly referred to as “a dreamer” during the fraternity's early years. Completed three years of pre-medical work before leaving the university. Occupation: not stated on the national site.
Henry Tourner Asher
Born June 30, 1890, Owensboro, Kentucky. Eldest of seven; the son of a Baptist Minister. He became an instructor at Lincoln Institute in Jefferson City, Missouri, from 1914-1915. Received the degree of LL.B. at the Detroit College of Law in 1928. An unassuming, dependable supporter of Negro youth, equal rights, higher education, and religious affairs.
Marcus Peter Blakemore
Born in Franklin, Indiana, on January 3, 1889. Eldest of four children. Affectionately known as “Blakie.” He organized the Electric Engineering Company, which he operated until he enlisted. Served in the U.S. Army as the rank of Private with the 30th Company, 154th Depot Brigade.
Guy Levis Grant
Born April 9, 1891, New Albany, Indiana. The third of thirteen children, five of whom became members of the fraternity. In 1920, he received a D.D.S. degree from Indiana Dental School, then a part of Indiana University. He practiced dentistry in Indianapolis for over 50 years. Was small in stature but was a giant in charitable endeavors and a preserver of history.
Paul Waymond Caine
Born in Greencastle, Indiana on May 17, 1890. Enrolled at Indiana University sometime between 1909 and 1910 as a business major. Was the consummate entrepreneur and chef before his time. He was a fine caterer and was in demand by the White fraternities on campus.
George Wesley Edmonds
Born in Knight Township, Vanderburgh County, Indiana, on August 13, 1890. Eldest of two sons. Was witty and an enigma to most in the fraternity. Edmonds was listed as the Corresponding Secretary in the articles of incorporation of the Grand Chapter of Kappa Alpha Nu. Occupation: not stated on the national site.
Ezra Dee Alexander
Born in Bloomington, Indiana, on July 18, 1891. Second eldest of seven children. Fondly known as “Dee.” He held positions as a teacher and principal at Indiana public schools. M.D., Indiana University Medical School, 1919. He practiced medicine in Indianapolis for nearly 50 years. Served in the Army Medical Corps during WWI and as a medical examiner during WWII.
Edward Giles Irvin
Born August 13, 1893, in Spencer, Indiana, and was the seventh of nine children of an African Methodist Episcopal minister. At only 17 years of age, Irvin was the youngest of ten Black students who founded Kappa Alpha Psi® Fraternity. Was a civic and religious leader, journalist, and entrepreneur.

The 5 Objectives of Kappa Alpha Psi

Each member of Kappa Alpha Psi commits to these core objectives

  1. To unite college men of culture, patriotism and honor in a bond of fraternity.
  2. To encourage honorable achievement in every field of human endeavor.
  3. To promote the spiritual, social, intellectual, and moral welfare of its members.
  4. To aid the aims and purposes of colleges and universities.
  5. To inspire service in the public interest.

Intake Policy

Membership is a solemn commitment. A lifelong dedication to the ideas and lofty purposes of Kappa Alpha Psi®.

Kappa Alpha Psi® is not interested in the simple accumulation of members and chapters. Its emphasis, however, is in the quality and integrity of its membership.

Stands firm in its commitment to achievement, brotherhood, leadership, and service—without the need for hazing.

Membership Intake Policy (Code of Conduct)
“It is the goal of the Fraternity to see that all individuals committing acts of hazing and other unlawful and Prohibited Acts are punished … to the full extent of the rules of the fraternity, college and the criminal law.” “The Fraternity has outlawed pledging and dispensed with the customary practice of having pledges or a protracted probationary period prior to one's initiation into full membership into the fraternity.” “The Fraternity has not only outlawed pledging, but bans from its membership those who submit to such a vile process …”

How the Provinces came to be
1920 – 3rd Grand Polemarch George F. David, II created a hierarchy modeled on the AME Church: three districts. 1923 – 4th Grand Polemarch W. Ellis Stewart renamed them regions, led by Regional Directors. 1926 – 5th Grand Polemarch Earl B. Dickerson renamed regions provinces. The title of “Regional Director” changed to “Province Polemarch.” Province Polemarchs serve as chief administrative officers for each region.

The Western Province

One of the original provinces established in 1923, the Western Province of Kappa Alpha Psi includes members in Alaska, Arizona, California, Hawaii, Idaho, Nevada, Oregon, Utah, Washington, Japan and South Korea.

57 active chapters across 9 Western U.S. States and two international chapters (Japan and South Korea).

The province manages its operations by breaking the area up into functional “clusters”: the Pacific Northwest, Northern California, Southern California, and the Southwest.

Province website: www.wp1911.com

Province history
The Provinces were formally called Regions. The fraternity held its 15th Grand Chapter Meeting December 26th thru 30th, 1925 in New York City. Grand Polemarch Earl B. Dickerson and the members assembled changed the designation of Regional Director to Province Polemarch. The first Polemarch of the Western Region—Judge Carl R. Johnson of Kansas City Missouri, and member of the Kansas City Alumni—served from 1925 until 1931. During those early years of the fraternity's history, the Western Region extended no further west than the states of Kansas, Oklahoma and Nebraska except for the Upsilon Chapter at University of California Los Angeles. On April 25, 1923 the fraternity chartered the Upsilon chapter on the campus of the University of California Los Angeles, the first chapter of the current Western Province. From 1923 to 1938, brothers who attended UCLA and remained in Los Angeles meet regularly and established the Los Angeles Alumni, chartered on August 8, 1938. The Los Angeles Alumni chapter continued to induct brothers through the Upsilon Chapter at both Undergraduate and Alumni levels.

Birthplace of Kappa League
Under the chairmanship of Brother Mel Davis and with the able assistance of Brother Edgar H. Bishop, the Los Angeles Alumni Chapter's Social Action/Guide Right Program took the form of a training activity for young men of the Alain Le Roy Locke High School in the late 1960's. The program was adopted by all chapters at the 56th Grand Chapter meeting in 1970. Brothers Mel Davis & Edgar Bishop presented the Kappa League Program at the 56th Grand Chapter Meeting in Charlotte, NC in 1970 for adoption by all chapters. The Congressman congratulated the Los Angeles Alumni Chapter for having started the Kappa League and the Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity for having adopted the idea as a national program. The entire proceedings were printed in the Congressional Record. Presently, the Kappa League/Guide Right Program is Los Angeles Alumni Chapter's major national community service project.
Birthplace of the Black & White Ball
We are honored to be the birthplace of the annual Black & White Ball - one of the most elegant social affairs in the nation. Brothers, visitors, and their guests from throughout the nation attend and offer rave reviews. Our annual Black & White Ball & Scholarship Fundraiser. This elegant evening supports our scholarship program while celebrating our rich legacy.

Programs & Leadership

Guide Right
Guide Right is our National Service Initiative and includes all our youth oriented programs. Conceived in 1922 by Leon W. Stewart, and suggested at the twelfth Grand Chapter of Kappa Alpha Psi®. Soon after the St. Louis Alumni Chapter's beginning in 1921. A program of the educational and occupational guidance of youth, primarily inspirational and informative in character. To place the training, experience and interest of successful men at the disposal of youth. WE ARE THE OLDEST, MOST SUCCESSFUL MENTORING PROGRAM FOR YOUNG PEOPLE OF COLOR! The Flagship Initiative of the Guide Right Service Program is the Kappa Leadership Development League.
Kappa League
Kappa League was founded on Thursday, February 12, 1969, by the Los Angeles Alumni Chapter at Alain LeRoy Locke High School. The founders of the Kappa Instructional Leadership League were Mel L. Davis and Edgar H. Bishop. Adopted by the Grand Chapter at the 56th Grand Chapter, which was held August 12-15, 1970. Kappa League is a program for the educational, occupational and social guidance of male students in grades 6th-12th. Kappa League is a subset of the Guide Right, our National Service Initiative. Five study areas: SELF-IDENTITY | Discipline, assurance, awareness and Fraternity. TRAINING | Academic, career choice, preparation and organization. COMPETITION | Politics and sports SOCIAL | Religion, the arts, entertainment, conversation, communication, etiquette, and manner. HEALTH EDUCATION | Physical fitness. And sex education, drugs, health and safety.

Membership Training Academy

In the chapter's words

Everything is voluntary
Nothing in this process is mandatory or required. Every step toward membership is your choice and completely up to you. We will only ever offer advice and recommendations. The decision is always yours.
Membership is earned, not given
Part of earning it is your own effort to get to know the members of our chapter and to support the public events the chapter hosts. Showing up benefits you. It demonstrates dedication and commitment, which are qualities we look for in potential members. It also gives the Brothers of the chapter a real opportunity to get to know you.
Relationships matter
If we do not know you, you will not have the support needed to become a member. Brothers will not vouch or vote for someone they have never met, and that support is crucial to the membership process. It is your responsibility to make the effort to get to know members of the Los Angeles (CA) Alumni Chapter of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc. We will be sharing a list of MTA certified Brothers you can reach out to, but in the meantime, you should be making every effort to meet Brothers on your own. While some of you may already know members of the Fraternity, your focus should be on members of the Los Angeles Alumni Chapter, as these are the men who will be voting on your membership. You should also be getting to know each other. Some of you may very well become line brothers, and the better you know one another now, the stronger that bond will be.
What to expect
Communication from the chapter will become more frequent from here on, and we ask that you respond and acknowledge messages in a timely manner. Meet chapter leadership, get to know your fellow interests, and learn about the requirements for membership and the next steps in the process. Make every effort to attend, be on time, and have your camera on. This is your opportunity to be seen. Please look for a separate email containing the calendar invite for our bi-weekly Zoom touch base meeting. When you receive it, please accept the invite so we can confirm it reached you.
From the October 14 touch base
Pass a 100-question test with 70% accuracy. Obtain the necessary documentation within two weeks of the informational meeting; a two-week period to complete applications and prepare for interviews. Members must respond to communications promptly. Missing more than two sessions or being late would result in dismissal from the process. Act, feel, and be as one within a line. All members equally knowledgeable about Kappa's information, including founders, Greek alphabet, and history. Learn basic Kappa Alpha Psi information: the 10 founders, their occupations, the Greek alphabet, and the organization's history. Review the national website, Western Province website, and LA Alumni website to familiarize yourself with the three levels of the organization. The distinction between founders and charter members. Dress professionally. Avoid the word “brother.” The organization does not haze. Importance of humility. New members should be willing to follow established traditions and processes before making changes. Attempts to join multiple chapters would be discovered through the portal system.
